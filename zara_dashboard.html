🏆 Insight 1: Jackets Dominate Revenue

Jackets generate $26.6M — 68.2% of total revenue despite being 55.6% of products. The top 9 of 10 highest-revenue products are jackets. This makes jackets the most critical product line to protect and grow.

⚠️ Insight 2: WOMAN Section Severely Underserved

Only 34 WOMAN products (13.5%) vs 218 MAN products. Revenue from WOMAN is just $3.3M (8.4%). Expanding the women's assortment is a massive untapped revenue opportunity.

✅ Insight 3: Price is the #1 Revenue Driver

Price and Revenue have a 0.75 correlation — the strongest of any variable. Raising average selling prices by 10–15% (with quality justification) could add ~$2.9M–$4.3M in revenue without needing more units sold.

📦 Insight 4: High Volume Despite High Prices

Price and Sales Volume correlation is only -0.07, meaning higher prices do NOT significantly hurt sales volume. Zara customers are price-insensitive — this supports a premium pricing strategy.

✅ Insight 5: Promotions Boost Revenue +15.8%

Promoted products average $166.6K vs $143.9K for non-promoted — a 15.8% lift. Interestingly, it's driven by higher priced products being promoted, not deeper discounts. Promotions attract premium buyers.

🎯 Insight 6: Sweet Spot Price Range = $60–$150

Products priced $60–$150 (140 products, 55.6%) generate $24.9M (63.9% of revenue). This is the optimal zone where price and volume balance perfectly for Zara.

⚠️ Insight 7: Jeans Category Hugely Underrepresented

Only 8 jeans products (3.2%) generating $860K. For a fashion brand of Zara's scale, this is remarkably thin. Expanding jeans SKUs could quickly capture significant market share.

✅ Insight 8: Front-of-Store Placement = Best ROI

Front-of-store products average 1,873 units sold vs 1,778 at end-caps (+5.3%). This proves that product placement strategy has measurable impact and should prioritize best-margin products.
